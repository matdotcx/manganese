# Manganese: The Deadline Colour System

Manganese starts in the print room. Charcoal ink (`#232323`) on warm cream paper (`#f8f5f0`), with corrections in grease-pencil orange (`#ff7900`).

The orange does most of the work. A grease pencil marks what matters and doesn't rub off, and that's its job here too.

## The rest of the palette

Three more colours round it out:

- **Maritime Blue** (`#0079ff`) for structure and information. Blueprint blue.
- **Botanical Green** (`#2a664d`) for success. The green of an old accounts ledger.
- **Warm Red** (`#cc3300`) for errors. Nobody needs red explained.

Each has one job, and none of them shouts over the orange.

## Two moods

Light is a bright studio first thing in the morning. Dark is the same desk after everyone's gone home.

The roles stay put between them. Green still means success, blue still means information and orange still means look here.

## Warm on purpose

Hours in front of a screen go better on cream than on white, and a tool you use every day should be nice to look at. That's the whole idea.

It won't suit everyone, and it doesn't need to.
