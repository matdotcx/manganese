# Manganese: The Deadline Colour System

Manganese began with a love for the craft of typography and a belief that our digital tools should feel as thoughtful as the best printed books.

## Inspired by Print

The foundation comes from traditional printing: the rich charcoal of quality ink (`#232323`), the warm cream of fine paper (`#f8f5f0`), and the vibrant orange of grease pencil markup (`#ff7900`). These aren't arbitrary choices—they're colours that have guided clear communication for generations.

There's something satisfying about working with colours that have history. The orange isn't just bright—it's the indelible colour of the grease pencil, the tool that marks what matters and won't be erased. The charcoal isn't just dark—it's the deep, complex colour of ink that was made to last. The paper isn't just light—it's the subtle warmth that makes reading comfortable for hours.

## Building on What Works

As the system grew, we added three more colours that extend this philosophy:

- **Maritime Blue** (`#0079ff`): For structure and information—the colour of technical drawings and blueprints
- **Botanical Green** (`#2a664d`): For growth and success—inspired by the deep greens of old accounting ledgers  
- **Warm Red** (`#cc3300`): For attention and errors—the instinctive colour of immediate concern

Each colour serves a purpose and works harmoniously with the others. They're not competing for attention—they're collaborating to create interfaces that feel both professional and human.

## Two Moods, Same Values

Manganese offers both light and dark themes because different times call for different environments. The light theme captures the feeling of working in a bright studio—clean, focused, energizing. The dark theme provides the concentrated comfort of evening work—warm, easy on the eyes, conducive to deep thinking.

Both themes maintain the same relationships between colours. A function is always green, information is always blue, emphasis is always orange. This consistency means you can switch between environments without losing the visual language you've learned.

## Warmth in Digital Spaces

What makes Manganese distinctive is its warmth. These colours invite you in rather than keeping you at a distance. They're sophisticated without being cold, professional without being sterile. They acknowledge that the best tools are the ones that feel good to use day after day.

The goal isn't just readability—though the colours meet high contrast standards. It's about creating an environment where you want to work, where the colours support your thinking rather than distracting from it.

## A Thoughtful Choice

Manganese represents the idea that every detail of our working environment matters. That choosing colours thoughtfully—considering their history, their relationships, their effect on mood and productivity—is worth the effort.

It's for people who appreciate when things are done with care, who notice the difference between something functional and something that's both functional and beautiful. It's one option among many, designed for those who value the intersection of craft, tradition, and modern functionality.
